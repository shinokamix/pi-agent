# Clarity

Produce accurate, useful prose that readers can understand, trust, and want to continue
reading.

## Priorities

When principles conflict, protect them in this order:

1. **Reader outcome:** the text does its job.
2. **Truth and meaning:** facts, constraints, intent, and uncertainty survive.
3. **Substance:** claims have information, mechanisms, examples, or reasons.
4. **Structure:** information arrives in a useful order.
5. **Clarity:** actors, actions, relations, and references are unambiguous.
6. **Voice and craft:** the prose fits its author, audience, medium, and moment.
7. **Concision:** nothing remains without a purpose.

Accuracy outranks simplicity. Necessary context outranks brevity. An established voice outranks
a generic preference. Break a lower-level rule rather than make the text less true, useful, or
natural.

## Establish the outcome

Determine from the request and context:

- who will read the text and what they already know;
- what they should understand, feel, decide, or do afterward;
- where the text will appear and how it will be read;
- what voice, terminology, and formality fit;
- which facts, examples, claims, and constraints are available.

For substantial text, form a success criterion: what should the reader be able to do or explain
after reading? Infer what is clear. Ask only when missing information would materially change
the result; do not turn a small request into an interview.

A text needs a primary job, not always a thesis. A guide leads to an outcome, a reference
supports lookup, an error helps recovery, and an argument defends a position.

## Non-negotiables

- Never invent a fact, name, number, date, quotation, citation, URL, command, result,
  benchmark, or personal experience.
- Never borrow authority from unnamed experts, studies, reports, or critics.
- Do not add, remove, or strengthen a factual claim without support.
- Preserve intent, constraints, position, terminology, and degree of certainty.
- If required information is unavailable, ask, narrow the claim, omit it, or mark the gap.
- Follow an author's sample or an established product style over this skill's defaults.
- Do not manufacture personality with typos, forced slang, staged candor, or invented memories.

## Writing pass

Apply these passes in order. Scale their depth to the task, but do not polish a later layer
while an earlier one is broken.

### 1. Substance

Identify the answer, request, result, claim, mechanism, decision, or experience the text exists
to convey. A polished sentence cannot rescue missing substance.

Each passage must add information, establish a relation, provide an example, create an intended
effect, or help the reader act. If it is fluent but empty, supply what is missing from available
sources, ask for it, or cut the passage. Never disguise the gap with smoother language.

Prefer concrete facts, actions, examples, and consequences to abstract claims of importance.
A detail must improve understanding, trust, memory, or action; otherwise it is decoration.

### 2. Order and structure

Lead with what best orients the reader: the result, situation, request, constraint, claim, or
first useful step. Do not restate the prompt, title, or importance of the topic.

Order the rest around the reader's next question or action, not the writer's discovery process.
Put prerequisites before instructions, decisions before supporting detail, and warnings before
the action that triggers them.

Give each section and paragraph one function. Keep related ideas together. Sequential prose
should develop from one paragraph to the next; reference material should favor independent,
searchable sections.

Use headings, lists, and tables only to help readers navigate, act, or compare. Formatting must
clarify content, not make thin content appear complete.

### 3. Sentences

- Name the actor when the actor matters.
- Prefer active voice when clearer; keep passive voice when the actor is unknown, irrelevant,
  or less important than the object.
- Prefer positive statements when more direct; keep negation when it carries meaning.
- Prefer familiar words unless another term is more precise for the reader.
- Keep subjects, verbs, modifiers, and referents close enough to prevent ambiguity.
- State cause, consequence, condition, contrast, and sequence when readers could infer the
  wrong relation.
- Express parallel ideas in parallel forms.
- Keep tense, point of view, terminology, and formality consistent unless a deliberate change
  serves the text.
- Split a sentence when the reader loses its subject or direction, not merely because it is
  long.

### 4. Craft, cut, and verify

Where the form allows it, build forward motion: each passage should answer, reveal, complicate,
or advance. Choose details that change understanding. Let a real position appear when needed.
Vary sentence shape and pace by function. Use emphasis, repetition, contrast, and imagery only
when the text earns them. Help the reader instead of performing empathy or enthusiasm.

Preserve characteristic phrasing, humor, restraint, uncertainty, and emotional temperature.
Utility copy may need no imagery or distinctive voice; authorial prose usually does.

Cut throat-clearing, prompt echoes, repeated conclusions, redundant qualifiers, empty
transitions, obvious explanations, and sections present only to satisfy a template. Concise
writing is not uniformly short: preserve useful detail, context, qualification, rhythm, and
emphasis. End on the last useful consequence, image, limitation, or action, then stop.

Finally compare facts, numbers, dates, names, links, commands, quotations, constraints, and
degrees of certainty with the source. Nothing may appear, disappear, or grow stronger by
accident.

## Required tests

Run these questions on the result:

- **Outcome:** Can the reader understand or do what the text is for?
- **Mechanism:** When something is called important, improved, robust, or transformative, what
  happens differently? Replace unsupported significance with the mechanism, result, or limit.
- **Actor and relation:** Is it clear who or what acts, what happens, and how adjacent claims
  relate? If a relation requires invention, the claims do not support each other.
- **Delete:** What would the reader lose if a passage disappeared? Information, logic, rhythm,
  emotional effect, and navigation count. If the answer is “nothing,” cut it.
- **Specificity:** Does each detail make the text more understandable, verifiable, memorable,
  or actionable? Never add unsupported precision.
- **Voice:** Does the wording fit this author or product, this reader, and this situation? Do
  not confuse natural with universally casual.
- **Integrity:** Do meaning, facts, constraints, and uncertainty still match the sources?

## Adapt to the form

Apply only what fits:

- **Messages and emails:** surface the situation, request, and next step; do not bury the
  request beneath courtesy or background.
- **UI copy and errors:** make the state and next action clear. Say what failed, what it affects,
  why if known, and how to recover when possible. Do not blame the user.
- **Procedures:** state prerequisites first, number required sequences, keep one main action per
  step, and include verification when success is not obvious.
- **Technical documentation:** keep tutorials, task-focused how-to guides, accurate reference,
  and conceptual explanation distinct unless combining them serves a clear reader need.
- **Landing pages and announcements:** connect a real need to concrete value, proof, or change.
  Put distinguishing information early and let calls to action name the action.
- **Arguments and authorial prose:** state the position when appropriate, address material
  limits and genuine objections, and protect specific perspective and language. Do not demand
  a personal voice from utilitarian text.

## Patterns to question

Inspect, but do not automatically ban: importance without mechanism; pseudo-specific anecdotes;
unnamed authority; generic openings and conclusions; repetition without progress;
meta-narration; balance that avoids a decision; unsupported promotion; decorative formatting;
mechanical sentence patterns; polished slogans where evidence should be; and performed empathy,
candor, humility, or excitement.

Revise a pattern only when it replaces information, obscures a relation, weakens trust, or
conflicts with the intended voice. Read the result once as a continuous piece, fix material
problems, and stop. Never optimize prose toward numeric targets, AI-detector scores, or a
uniform idea of style.
